Current Account of the Balance of Payments

Unit 6.3 in Chapter 6: International economic issues

EXPLAIN HOW A HIGH RATE OF INFLATION AND A RISE IN AN ECONOMY'S EXCHANGE RATE CAN EACH CAUSE A DEFICIT IN AN ECONOMY'S CURRENT ACCOUNT OF THE BALANCE OF PAYMENTS

SOURCE: CAIE, ECONOMICS, 9708, PAPER 2, VARIANT 2, MAY / JUNE, 2018, QUESTION __, 12 MARKS

Model Answer written by Anuj:

AO1 (2 MARKS)

Inflation refers to a rise in general prices. A rise in exchange rate describes appreciation, which means that the economy’s currency can buy more of another’s. The current account is a component of the balance of payments that tracks all the transactions of goods and services as well as income and transfer payments between citizens of a country and residents abroad.

AO2 (6 MARKS)

A high rate of inflation will increase domestic prices, including the prices of of exports. They become relatively more expensive for foreign buyers, leading to a decrease in the quantity demanded for exports especially if demand is price elastic. Conversely, the prices of imports are less affected by domestic inflation so there will be a relative increase in quantity demanded for imports. This causes a current account deficit consequently.

An appreciation of the exchange rate can make exports more expensive and imports cheaper which can make the economy’s export-orientated firms less competitive internationally. The demand for imports and therefore expenditure on imports will rise whereas the demand for exports and therefore the revenue generated by exports will decrease. This leads to a negative balance of trade and causes a deficit on the current account.

AO3 (4 MARKS)

It is important to consider the relative rate of inflation between the domestic economy and its trading partners. If the trading partner’s inflation is greater than the domestic inflation rate, there may be a current account surplus rather than a deficit.

If the Marshall-Lerner condition is not met (where the price elasticity of demand for imports and exports s less than 1), then a currency appreciation may worsen the current account balance as the decrease in export revenue may be outweighed by the increase in import expenditure. However, an appreciation might improve the current account balance if the elasticities are greater than 1.

The J-curve effect can be used to explain the impacts on the current account on different time scales. Initially after a currency appreciation, the current account effects are not apparent immediately. This is because, in the short term, existing export contracts may not decrease the volume of exports significantly. Similarly, consumers and businesses may not immediately adjust their import behavior in response to lower prices. There may be a short-term deterioration but long-term improvement in the current account, because in the long run, exporters may negotiate new contracts and seek new markets, maintaining competitiveness.

In conclusion, generally speaking, a high rate of inflation and a rise in an economy’s exchange rate can cause a deficit in the current account, but there may be long-term improvements with regards to appreciation.
