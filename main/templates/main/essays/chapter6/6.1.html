The Reasons for International Trade

Unit 6.1 in Chapter 6: International economic issues

EXPLAIN THE BENEFITS OF FREE TRADE AS SUGGESTED BY THE PRINCIPLE OF COMPARATIVE ADVANTAGE. [8]

SOURCE: CAIE, ECONOMICS, 9708, PAPER 2, VARIANT 3, MAY / JUNE, 2019

VIDEO REFERENCE LINK

Podcast:

Model Answer written by Anuj:

AO1 (2 MARKS)

Free trade is when trade occurs between countries without restrictions or barriers. Comparative advantage is the idea that two countries should specialise in goods and services they can produce at a lower opportunity cost compared to another country.

AO2 (4 MARKS)

An important benefit of free trade is the increase in world efficiency due to a more efficient allocation of resources where countries have their comparative advantage. The basic economic problem is solved by maximising net benefit to both consumers and producers. There is also increased choice for consumers and businesses.

Raw materials can be sourced from all around at the cheapest prices. Businesses can lower prices, gain greater market share and enjoy higher profitability. Consumer welfare and standards of living improve.

AO3 (2 MARKS)

There is a slight risk of over-specialisation and a decline in industries that rely on comparative advantage too much.

In conclusion, in an ever-increasing globalised world, free trade and making use of comparative advantage reap many benefits because everyone benefits from trade.

DISCUSS WHETHER A COUNTRY SHOULD BE MORE CONCERNED BY A FALL IN ITS TERMS OF TRADE OR A FALL IN ITS DOMESTIC PRICE LEVEL. [12]

Model Answer written by Yijun:

AO1 (2 MARKS)

Terms of trade are measured by the ratio of the index of export prices to the index of import prices. In the real world, international trade involves many countries and numerous products; therefore, a country’s terms of trade are often computed as an index number.

AO2 (6 MARKS)

A fall in the index of a country’s Terms of Trade (TOT), also known as unfavourable movement, is attributed to decreasing prices of its exports, and/or increasing prices of its imports. It means that the country can import less in return for any given amount of exports. This will reduce the international purchasing power of a country; however, it may benefit or harm the country in many different ways.

Advantage (Elastic)

A fall in TOT will have a detrimental effect on trade of goods and employment in the country. The reason for this is that a fall in relative prices of exports may, assuming a high price elasticity of demand, lead to a rise in total value of export sales, whereas an increase in prices of imports will make them relatively unattractive, so imports decline. In both cases, trade balance improves and employment may rise in the export industry.

Disadvantage (Inelastic)

However, when international demand for the country’s exports is price inelastic, the given fall in prices of exports will generate a proportionately small rise in its quantity exported, thus resulting in a fall in export earnings and worsening trade balance. Similarly, if imports are demanded inelastically, this will continue to worsen the deficit, also cause cost-push inflation when raw materials are imported and reduce employment.

Deflation is defined as a sustained period where general price levels fall. It is normally associated with falling levels of AD, leading to a negative output gap where actual GDP falls short of potential GDP.

Disadvantage

It is an indicator of declining economic activity. In recession, falling AD drives prices down. Firms are forced to cut down production, and this leads to unemployment. Household incomes fall and AD falls more. This results in a decline in all types of spending, resulting in level economic activities to decrease. The real value of household, corporate, and government debt rises when price falls - another factor that continues to reduce spending.

Advantage

Definition is not necessarily bad! If falling prices are caused by higher productivity, then it can go hand in hand with robust growth. It will also benefit the trade balance as it increases competitiveness of exports and reduces levels of imports. Nevertheless, this is subject to elasticity of exports and income elasticity of demand for imports

AO3 (4 MARKS)

The discussion suggests that the negative impact of deflation is more extensive and harmful than worsening TOT. Many countries, such as China, economically thrive with unfavourable TOT and have been able to cope with it by implementing appropriate policies. While coping with deflation is relatively more complicated and requires more dynamic set of policies.
