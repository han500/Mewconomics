Protectionism

Unit 6.2 in Chapter 6: International economic issues

DISCUSS THE ARGUMENTS THAT ARE USED TO JUSTIFY PROTECTIONISM AND CONSIDER WHETHER THESE ARGUMENTS CAN EVER BE JUSTIFIED.

SOURCE: CAIE, ECONOMICS, 9708, PAPER 2, VARIANT 2, MAY / JUNE, 2019, QUESTION __, 12 MARKS

Model Answer written by Anuj:

AO1 (2 MARKS)

Protectionism is any barrier that restricts free trade to protect domestic producers from foreign competition. This involves interference with market forces or the relative prices of imports and exports.

AO2 (6 MARKS)

One justification for protectionism is to protect infant industries. These small industries do not have the economies of scale to compete with large foreign industries. Protectionism allows these small industries to grow over time and benefit from economies of scale to reduce the costs of production and compete with more established industries abroad.

Another justification is to protect against dumping which is the sale of a product in overseas markets at a price below the cost of production often due to heavy subsidies or minimum price schemes generating an excess supply. Dumped products are artificially more competitive than domestic products, leading to structural unemployment and political unrest.

Furthermore, protectionism is adopted to eliminate a current account deficit and promote economic growth. Imported goods are either more expensive or restricted, reducing import expenditure, which ceteris paribus improves the trade balance.

AO3 (4 MARKS)

While protecting infant industries is important for developing countries looking to diversify and reduce the risk of primary commodity over-specialisation, it may fail in the medium to long term because of a possible rise in complacency. This may reduce competitiveness.

Even though dumping is an important issue to deal with, it is very hard to prove because of incomplete information regarding the costs of production. The accused country may feel targeted and retaliate heavily leading to a tit-for-tat protectionism that benefits no one.

The biggest problem with using protectionism to close trade deficits is retaliation. This may be implemented much more strongly, reducing export revenues more so than the import expenditure saved, which would lead to a worsening of the trade deficit.

Of course, there are undeniable benefits of free trade as well. Greater specialisation increases world efficiency and businesses have the potential to access larger economies of scale. Greater market sizes also mean faster rates of GDP growth and technology transfers.

In conclusion, protectionism is valid in many scenarios. A thorough cost-benefit analysis of different protectionist methods should be done to weigh out the necessity for protectionist measures, considering the long-run.
