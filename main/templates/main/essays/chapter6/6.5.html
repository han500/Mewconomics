Policies to Correct Imbalances in the Current Account of the Balance of Payments

Unit 6.3 in Chapter 6: International economic issues

DISCUSS WHETHER EXPENDITURE-REDUCING POLICIES ARE LIKELY TO REDUCE CURRENT ACCOUNT DEFICIT ON THE BALANCE OF PAYMENT WITH A FLOATING EXCHANGE RATE.

SOURCE: CAIE, ECONOMICS, 9708, PAPER 2, VARIANT 1, OCTOBER / NOVEMBER, 2021, QUESTION __, 12 MARKS

Model Answer written by Ethan Lam:

AO1 (2 MARKS)

Expenditure reducing policies are done by the government with the aim to reduce overall spending in the economy and reduce imports via contractionary fiscal and monetary policies. A freely floating exchange rate occurs when the government allows the exchange rate to be determined purely by market forces and there is no attempt to ask the central bank to influence the external value of the exchange rate and thus expenditure reducing policy for correcting current account deficit may be effective since currency is flexible based on the supply and demand for the currency.

AO2 (6 MARKS)

Contractionary fiscal policy means that the government could mix taxes such as income tax, corporate tax and indirect tax such as sales tax and VAT (Value-Added Tax). Governments can also reduce spending on infrastructure and unemployment benefits, this influences people to have less purchasing power, thus spending on local goods and imports both fall. Hence, aggregate demand (AD) and inflation both fall as well. Also, tax revenue for the government will rise and the government budget will be in surplus. Direct taxes are quicker and easier to implement than indirect taxes.

Contractionary monetary policies means government through Central Bank will increase interest rates and reduce money supply. High interest rate will encourage savings and increase cost of borrowing, reducing spending on imports. Central banks will also restrict granting of loans to reduce money supply, which reduces local consumption, thus causing AD and inflation rates to fall. This causes exports to become cheaper, reducing the current account deficit.

However, expenditure reducing policies will reduce AD and leads to a fall in National Income (GDP) of the local economy. Thus, expenditure switching policies may be used to correct current account deficit as it will not affect local demand and sales. For example, the government may opt to use protectionist policies such as tariffs, embargoes, exchange controls and quotas to reduce imports to increase local demand and reduce import demand, reducing current account deficit. However, other countries may retaliate and reduce imports from the country, thus export revenue may fall and current account deficit may not resolve. If there is inflation and current account deficit at the same time, expenditure reducing policies should be used to reduce AD, driving down demand-pull inflation.

AO3 (4 MARKS)

Still, if there is high preference for import (inelastic PED for imports) and low confidence in domestic goods, high tax rates and interest rates, low money supply and government spending are not likely to be effective. Similarly, if speculation and confidence for domestic currency is high, foreign investors will still purchase domestic currency and thus exchange rate will be high. This may cause the further appreciation of a currency and will lead to relatively expensive exports, and current account deficits may instead exacerbate as a result of this.

Expenditure-reducing policies may also lead to deflation then recession and high unemployment if used for long periods of time. Expenditure-reducing policy may also take time to be effective.

The J-curve effect can be used to explain the impacts on the current account on different time scales. Initially after a currency appreciation, the current account effects are not apparent immediately. This is because, in the short term, existing export contracts may not decrease the volume of exports significantly. Similarly, consumers and businesses may not immediately adjust their import behavior in response to lower prices. There may be a short-term deterioration but long-term improvement in the current account, because in the long run, exporters may negotiate new contracts and seek new markets, maintaining competitiveness.

In conclusion, expenditure-reducing policy may reduce the current account deficit for floating exchange rate economies, but should be accompanied with other policies to remain effective in the long run.
