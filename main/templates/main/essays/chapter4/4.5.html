Unemployment

Unit 4.5 in Chapter 4: The Macroeconomy

EXPLAIN HOW UNEMPLOYMENT AND INFLATION ARE MEASURED.

SOURCE: CAIE, ECONOMICS, 9708, PAPER 2, VARIANT 3, OCTOBER / NOVEMBER, 2014, QUESTION __, 8 MARKS

Model Answer written by Hilary:

AO1 (2 MARKS)

Unemployment is used to define the state of being jobless but actively seeking work. Inflation is the continuous rise in general price levels over a period of time.

AO2 (4 MARKS)

Unemployment is measured primarily through the unemployment rate, this is the number of workers in an area or economy without a job and looking for work, divided by the total number of workers in the labour force. One measure of unemployment is the claimant count, which is based on the number of people claiming unemployment benefits. Another measure of unemployment is found in employment surveys, they provide a broader and more comprehensive measure of unemployment, which includes those not eligible for unemployment benefits.

Inflation is primarily measured by the Consumer Price Index (CPI), which measures the percentage change in the price of a basket of goods and services consumed by households in a given period of time. These goods and services include food, housing, clothing, healthcare, education, transportation, and so on. Besides this, the GDP Deflator method can be used as it measures the level of prices of all new, domestically produced goods and services in an economy.
