Classification of Goods and Services

Unit 1.6 in Chapter 1: Basic economic ideas and resources

USING EXAMPLES, EXPLAIN WHY MERIT GOODS ARE SAID TO BE UNDERCONSUMED AND DEMERIT GOODS ARE OVERCONSUMED.

SOURCE: CAIE, ECONOMICS, 9708, PAPER 2, VARIANT 3, OCTOBER / NOVEMBER, 2022, QUESTION 2A, 8 MARKS

Model Answer written by Bryan Yeo:

AO1 (2 MARKS)
Define and show knowledge/understanding of the difference between merit and demerit goods

Merit goods are goods that, when consumed, generate positive externalities and are goods that are perceived to be desirable and beneficial for consumers. Demerit goods are goods that generate negative externalities when consumed and are perceived to be undesirable and harmful for consumers, more so than they may realise themselves.

AO2 (3 MARKS)

Merit goods are said to be underconsumed due to information failure, a condition where parties have a lack of information when making economic decisions. As a result, consumers may not be fully aware of the positive externalities generated when consuming merit goods, underestimating their value and benefits, which causes them to be overpriced and underconsumed. For example, education is a merit good that is oftentimes underconsumed because consumers are unaware of the benefits associated with education (such as higher chances of employment) and, as a result, may not be willing to invest in education. The overpriced nature of education may also render it unaffordable for many, such as those on low incomes, which again contributes to its underconsumption.

Demerit goods are said to be overconsumed also because of information failure. Consumers are not fully aware of the dangers of consuming excessive amounts of demerit goods, which leads to them being underpriced and overconsumed as consumers overestimate the private benefits gained from consuming these goods. For example, demerit goods such as cigarettes are overconsumed due to consumers not being fully aware of their negative implications, such as the increased risk of contracting diseases such as cancer, and some consumers also continue to consume these goods even if they are aware of the consequences linked to the increased consumption of demerit goods because some of these goods are addictive and habit-forming, smoking cigarettes being a prime example of this.

AO3 (3 MARKS)
No evaluation included in the mark scheme, but include anyways to be prudent

While information failure undoubtedly plays a key role in causing the underconsumption and overconsumption of merit and demerit goods, respectively, there are also cases where consumers are aware of the benefits of consuming merit goods and the consequences of consuming demerit goods but may still continue to underconsume and overconsume merit and demerit goods, respectively, for a range of reasons, such as a low income, the addictive nature of demerit goods, overpriced merit goods, and underpriced demerit goods. Hence, government intervention is required to address these issues to ensure the optimal consumption of these goods, such as the introduction of taxes to regulate the consumption of demerit goods to lower their consumption and production and subsidies to lower the prices of merit goods to make them more affordable to consumers to increase their production and consumption. In conclusion, the prime reason for the underconsumption of merit goods and overconsumption of demerit goods can be ascribed to information failure, and to ensure the optimal consumption of merit goods, government intervention may be needed to address information failures and other market failures to maximise social welfare.
