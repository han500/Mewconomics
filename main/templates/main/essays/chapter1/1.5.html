Production Possibility Curve

Unit 1.5 in Chapter 1: Basic economic ideas and resources

DISCUSS WHETHER AN OUTWARD SHIFT IN AN ECONOMY'S PPC CURVE IS MORE LIKELY TO OCCUR IN A FREE MARKET ECONOMY OR CENTRALLY PLANNED ECONOMY

SOURCE: CAIE, ECONOMICS, 9708, PAPER 2, VARIANT 3, OCTOBER / NOVEMBER, 2015, QUESTION 2B, 12 MARKS

VIDEO REFERENCE LINK

Model Answer written by Arissa Hani:

AO1 (4 MARKS)

The Production Possibilty Curve (PPC) is a curve that shows the maximum potential output of one good given the output of all other goods in an economy at a given point in time. An outward shift of PPC means an increase in productive capacity. For instance, economic growth will make the PPC curve shift outward.

AO2 (4 MARKS)

A free market economy is an economy that uses the laws of price mechanism and efficient allocation of resources to increase output. Not to mention, the economy is efficient through competition and strong incentives for enterprises to innovate and be creative in producing goods and services. Conversely, a centrally planned economy is an economy where the allocation of resources is decided by the government; there is also no competition among firms. As a result of this, their production of standardised goods is low in quality, due to a lack of incentive among firms to improve their goods to compete against each other.

AO3 (4 MARKS)

In a market economy, the allocation of resources is more efficient, and no wastage occurs. Firms also respond quickly to price mechanisms when supply and demand change, resulting in a competitive environment that encourages market firms to produce the greatest number of high-quality products with the resources they have available to meet demand. However, the market economy may not have sufficient funds for RnD and major technological advancement as private sectors are more limited in resources and often do not consider the benefit for the nation as their aim is to maximise profit. Meanwhile, centrally planned economies may not be as effective in their resource allocation because these types of economies are too complex, with no similar types of goods and services caused by no competition between firms. Moreover, the conditions of markets change all the time, so the absence of a quick feedback system leads to wastage and shortages. However, central planning always emphasises their aim to maximise welfare for the nation, so the supply of public goods will always be sufficient for consumption. Therefore, central planning may be more likely to shift PPC outward, considering the government is equal in distributing goods and services to the nation.

In conclusion, it is difficult to say which is better at shifting PPC outwards. The market economy is efficient, but it also has a lot of market failures, such as underproduction of merit goods. But all things said, the market economy does prove to be a better performer in this area compared to the central planning economy.
