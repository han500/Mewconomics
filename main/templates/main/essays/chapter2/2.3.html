Price Elasticity of Supply

Unit 2.3 in Chapter 2: The price system and the microeconomy

EXPLAIN TWO FACTORS THAT DETERMINE THE PRICE ELASTICITY SUPPLY OF A MANUFACTURED PRODUCT.

SOURCE: CAIE, ECONOMICS, 9708, PAPER 2, VARIANT 1, MAY / JUNE, 2022, QUESTION 3A, 8 MARKS

Model Answer written by Choo Pei Qi:

AO1 (2 MARKS)

Price elasticity of supply (PES) is a measure of a firm's responsiveness to a change in quantity supplied to a change in price. PES can be elastic (magnitude greater than 1) or inelastic (magnitude less than 1). It is the ratio of the percentage change in quantity supplied (QS) to the percentage change in price of a product (P).

AO2 (4 MARKS)

Factors that determine the PES include the time required to produce the product. Products that can be manufactured quickly tend to have a more elastic supply (PES > 1). For example, a company producing electronic gadgets with capital intensive, automated production lines can rapidly increase output in response to a rise in price, indicating a high PES. However, products that take longer to manufacture, such as custom-built equipment, will have less elastic supply due to the longer production time and less flexible technology (PES < 1).

Another factor is the existence of spare capacity for goods. It indicates the availability of unused resources or factors of production that can be quickly mobilised to increase supply as a response to an increase in price. When a firm has a high level of spare capacity, it can ramp up production swiftly at relatively low cost in response to an increase in price, resulting in a more price elastic supply. For example, a clothing manufacturer with underutilised machines and workers can quickly increase production if the price of clothing rises. Conversely, if a firm is operating near full capacity, any increase in production will likely require significant investment in new machinery, hiring additional workers, or overtime pay. This makes it more difficult and costly to respond to price changes, resulting in a less elastic supply.
[other: production time, factor mobility, perishability, comparative time period]

AO3 (2 MARKS)

To conclude, while these two factors are critical, it is essential to recognise that other elements such as the ability to store products and the mobility of factors of production also play a role in determining the PES of a manufactured product. Additionally, industry-specific characteristics and external economic conditions can further influence how responsive supply is to price changes.
