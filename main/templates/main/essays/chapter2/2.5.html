Consumer and Producer Surplus

Unit 2.5 in Chapter 2: The price system and the microeconomy

WITH THE HELP OF A DIAGRAM(S), EXPLAIN WHAT IS MEANT BY CONSUMER SURPLUS AND PRODUCER SURPLUS AND CONSIDER WHETHER A RISE IN THE PRICE OF A PRODUCT BECAUSE OF HIGHER COSTS OF PRODUCTION IS LIKELY TO ALWAYS REDUCE THE CONSUMER SURPLUS.

SOURCE: CAIE, ECONOMICS, 9708, PAPER 2, VARIANT 2, MAY / JUNE, 2023, QUESTION 2A, 8 MARKS

Model Answer written by Choo Pei Qi:

AO1 (3 MARKS)

Consumer surplus (CS) is the difference between the price a customer is willing to pay for a good or service and its market price. It is represented as the area under the demand curve, above the equilibrium price. Producer surplus (PS) is the difference between the price a supplier is willing to accept and what is actually paid. It is represented as the area above the supply curve, below the equilibrium price.

AO2 (3 MARKS)

An increase in production costs shifts the supply curve leftward (SS1→SS2), leading to a new equilibrium with a higher price (P1→P2) and lower quantity (Q1→Q2), as seen in Diagrams 1 and 2. However, the extent of the decrease in consumer surplus differs between the two. When demand is inelastic, the reduction in consumer surplus is smaller, as seen in Diagram 1, because consumers are less responsive to price changes, leading to a minor decrease in the quantity demanded. Consumers continue to purchase goods like necessities despite higher prices. However, when demand is elastic, as shown in diagram 2, there is a significant reduction in consumer surplus because consumers are highly responsive to price changes, leading to a substantial decrease in quantity demanded. This is normally the case for luxury goods, which take up a large portion of income.

AO3 (2 MARKS)

To conclude, a rise in the price of a product due to higher production costs leads to a reduction in consumer surplus. However, the extent of this reduction varies depending on the elasticity of demand. For elastic goods, consumer surplus decreases significantly, while for inelastic goods, the decrease is comparatively minor.
