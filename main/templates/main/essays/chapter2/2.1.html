Demand and Supply Curves

Unit 2.1 in Chapter 2: The price system and the microeconomy

WITH THE HELP OF A DIAGRAM, EXPLAIN WHAT COULD CAUSE AN INCREASE IN DEMAND FOR A PRODUCT AND CONSIDER WHETHER THE IMPACT OF AN INCREASE IN DEMAND ON THE PRICE OF THE PRODUCT WILL BE THE SAME IN THE SHORT RUN AND THE LONG RUN.

SOURCE: CAIE, ECONOMICS, 9708, PAPER 2, VARIANT 1, OCTOBER / NOVEMBER, 2023, QUESTION 2A, 8 MARKS

Model Answer written by Choo Pei Qi :

AO1 (3 MARKS)

An accurate demand and supply diagram with correctly labelled axes (1), correctly labelled shift of the demand curve to the right (1) with equilibrium positions in terms of both price and quantity clearly shown or a movement from E1 to E2 (1).

AO2 (3 MARKS)

As shown in the short-run and long-run diagram, as demand increases (DD1→DD2), the price increases (P1→P2) along with the quantity traded (Q1→Q2), hence a movement along the supply curve (E1→E2). An increase in demand (DD1→DD2) for a product can be caused by several factors.

For one, the increase in income of consumers. When consumers' income rises, they have more disposable income to spend, which increases the demand for normal goods. For example, with higher salaries, consumers are able to buy more clothes or electronics, hence increasing the demand for the respective products.

Another factor for increase in demand is the increase in price of a substitute good. Consumers will switch to the product in question, increasing its demand. For example, if the price of coffee increases significantly, consumers will switch to tea as an alternative.

Changing tastes and preferences can also have a significant effect on demand for products. Persuasive advertising is designed to cause change in taste and preferences which can cause an increase in demand. If a health study claims moisturiser is good for skincare, the demand for it will increase.

AO3 (3 MARKS)

Despite that, the extent of increase in price differs between short-run and long-run.

In the short run, the supply of a product is relatively inelastic because producers cannot quickly change the quantity produced. A sudden increase in demand will typically lead to a higher price as the immediate supply cannot meet the new level of demand. This is due to factors like fixed capacity, production constraints and time leafs in increasing production.

Nevertheless, in the long run, supply is more elastic as producers have more time to adjust their production levels. They can invest in new technologies, expand their facilities or employ more labour. Hence, the price increase will not be as pronounced as in the short run because the supply can increase to meet the higher demand. The exact impact on price in the long run is dependent on the nature of the industry, ability to scale production along with the availability and mobility of factors of production.

To conclude, an increase in demand usually leads to higher prices in both the short and long run. However, the extent of the increase in price relies on the price elasticity of supply.
