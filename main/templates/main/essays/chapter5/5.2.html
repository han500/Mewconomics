Fiscal Policy

Unit 5.2 in Chapter 5: Government macroeconomic intervention

DISCUSS WHETHER SUPPLY-SIDE POLICY IS MORE LIKELY TO BE SUCCESSFUL THAN FISCAL POLICY WHEN AN ECONOMY IS FACED WITH INFLATION

SOURCE: CAIE, ECONOMICS, 9708, PAPER 2, VARIANT 2, MAY / JUNE, 2017, QUESTION __, 12 MARKS

Model Answer written by Anuj:

AO1 (2 MARKS)

A fiscal policy involves changes in government spending and taxation to influence aggregate demand in an economy. A supply-side policy refers to any government action taken to shift the LRAS to influence its productive potential. Inflation is a sustained increase in general price levels over a period of time.

AO2 (6 MARKS)

A contractionary fiscal policy aims to decrease aggregate demand (AD) so that price levels decrease as well. In this case, the aim of fiscal policy is to shift the demand curve to the left. The marginal rate of income tax can be increased to reduce disposable income and reduce the incentive for consumption, decreasing AD. Another way is by increasing corporate tax, which is a tax on business profits. This decreases retained profits and reduces the marginal propensity to invest, decreasing AD. Moreover, governments can also reduce spending on infrastructure and public sector wages, significantly reducing AD due to a negative multiplier effect whereby an initial decrease in spending (AD) will decrease incomes in the economy, facilitating further rounds of saving.

On the other hand, supply-side policies aim to shift the LRAS curve outward, increasing real output in the long run. This can be done through investing in research and development. Through doing this, firms are able to produce higher-quality goods at lower prices while simultaneously lowering the cost of production and making production processes more efficient. Furthermore, government investment in upskilling and education can help boost labour productivity, which further boosts the real output of an economy and shifts the LRAS curve outward. Through shifting the LRAS curve outwards, prices are reduced in the long run, and inflationary pressures are also reduced.

AO3 (4 MARKS)

To evaluate, it must be made clear that government macroeconomic intervention must be taken only if there is a high, unstable rate of inflation. A low, stable inflation rate is a macroeconomic objective. While inflation is a key macroeconomic objective, contractionary fiscal policies may cause a shock in the economy and widen income inequalities if regressive taxes are raised and spending on welfare is cut. Austerity policies could worsen long-term economic prospects because improvements in education and infrastructure can generate strong returns over time, tax revenue can be generated, and productivity can be boosted, reducing inflation in the long run. Besides, the initial level of economic activity matters as well. If there is a large negative output gap (a large level of spare capacity), then contractionary fiscal policies will be of little use because a decrease in AD will be more consequential for real output than inflationary pressures. Thus, when there are low levels of spare capacity, there will be a much greater relief of pressure on the limited factors of production, leading to a much greater decrease in demand-pull inflation. Moreover, Laffer argued that by raising tax revenues, there will come a point where tax revenue goes down as tax rates increase because there is a disincentive for workers to work for higher incomes that will be taxed heavily. Tax evasion would be promoted, and there may be emigration, which could worsen the economy instead of improving it

Regarding supply-side, the main issue is that they usually take long periods to take effect on productivity, which is useless if the inflationary pressure is urgent or short term. They are not guaranteed to work either because there is an unintended possible consequence of firms misusing the funds, where they may use them to pay greater dividends to shareholders instead of R&D. Controversial measures, such as reducing benefits, can harm key stakeholders. Arguably, the main evaluation is derived from the fact that inflationary pressures can either be demand- or supply-side. Fiscal policies cannot deal with cost-push inflation, whereas supply-side cannot deal with demand-pull inflation.
