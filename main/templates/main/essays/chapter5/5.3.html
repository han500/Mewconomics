Monetary Policy

Unit 5.3 in Chapter 5: Government macroeconomic intervention

DISCUSS THE LIKELY CONSEQUENCES FOR AN ECONOMY OF AN INCREASE IN THE MONEY SUPPLY.

SOURCE: CAIE, ECONOMICS, 9708, PAPER 2, VARIANT 1, MAY / JUNE, 2018, QUESTION __, 12 MARKS

Model Answer written by Anuj:

AO1 (2 MARKS)

Increasing the money supply is a form of government expansionary monetary policy, and it can be achieved in several ways. One way is open market operations, which involves the purchase of government bonds that inject money into the banking system, leading to an increase in lending capacity. Another way is quantitative easing, which involves the purchase of long-term securities to boost the money supply. Another common method is to lower the interest rate to lower the return on savings, encouraging borrowing and spending, leading to an increase in the money supply as credit creation expands.

AO2 (6 MARKS)

If the money supply exceeds the economy’s capacity to produce goods and services (AD exceeds AS), then there will be demand-pull inflation. With more money in circulation, consumers have higher purchasing power, and prices may rise as rising demand outstrips supply. There may also be cost-push inflation if firms face higher costs of production due to increased wages (if labour demands higher wages due to higher purchasing power) or prices of raw materials (if there is a shortage, then the cost to obtain raw materials rises). Firms may pass the higher costs onto consumers, leading to inflation even without a rise in demand.

Moreover, when the money supply increases, it can lead to expectations of higher inflation rates in the future. This may erode the purchasing power of the currency, depreciating the currency. A weaker currency leads to imported inflation and can contribute to higher inflationary pressures.

AO3 (4 MARKS)

However, if the increase in money supply is matched by an increase in AS, the economy may be able to produce enough goods and services to meet the higher demand without significant inflation. For instance, increasing the money supply is accompanied by an improvement in labour productivity or productive capacity. At this equilibrium, there won’t be any bottlenecks in production.

Moreover, the consequences of an increase in money supply depend on the economic state. In a recession, there is a negative output gap and greater spare capacity to meet the rise in demand with a lower risk of inflation.

The magnitude of the money supply also matters. A small, controlled increase in the money supply will mitigate the risk of inflationary pressures better, reducing the risk of runaway inflation (an uncontrollable increase in general price levels).

In conclusion, this monetary policy tool has several consequences for an economy. Depending on the economic condition and the size of the increase, the consequences may be positive but there is a risk of unmanageable negative consequences.
