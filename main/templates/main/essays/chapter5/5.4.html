Unit 5.4 in Chapter 5: Government macroeconomic intervention

A GOVERNMENT EMBARKS ON A NEW SUPPLY-SIDE POLICY INVOLVING SUBSTANTIAL ADDITIONAL FUNDING FOR NEW ROAD SCHEMES AND FOR NEW TRAINING SCHEMES DESIGNED TO RAISE LABOUR PRODUCTIVITY. USE AN AD/AS DIAGRAM TO EXPLAIN THE EFFECTS OF THIS FUNDING ON THE LEVEL OF OUTPUT IN THE ECONOMY.

SOURCE: CAIE, ECONOMICS, 9708, PAPER 2, VARIANT 3, OCTOBER / NOVEMBER, 2020, QUESTION __, 8 MARKS

Model Answer written by Anuj:

AO1 (2 MARKS)

For an accurate AD/AS diagram that shows national output in equilibrium

In an AD/AS diagram, the national output is initially in equilibrium at the point where the AD and AS curves intersect. Aggregate demand (AD) represents the total quantity of goods and services demanded at different price levels in an economy. Aggregate supply (AS) refers to the total quantity of goods and services that firms are willing and able to produce at different price levels.

In the diagram, the AD curve is downward-sloping, indicating an inverse relationship between price level and real GDP. The AS curve is upward-sloping in the short run. Equilibrium occurs at the point where AD intersects AS, indicating the level of output and price in the economy before any changes in government policy.

AO2 (3 MARKS)

For a clear understanding of aggregate demand using an accurate diagram to show that the AD curve will shift to the right because of the increase in government spending and that this will increase the level of national output.

The training schemes funded by the government are designed to improve labour productivity. Higher productivity allows firms to produce more output with the same level of input, effectively increasing the economy’s capacity to produce goods and services. This results in a rightward shift of the AS curve from AS1 to AS2 in the long run, reflecting an improvement in the productive potential of the economy.

In the diagram, the rightward shift of the AS curve increases national output further, from Y2 to Y3, while reducing or stabilizing the price level as production becomes more efficient (P2 to P3). The final effect is a higher level of output without inflationary pressure, as the economy can produce more efficiently.

AO3 (2 MARKS)

The initial impact of increased government spending shifts the AD curve to the right, raising national output in the short run. Over time, improved labour productivity shifts the AS curve to the right, further increasing output and stabilising the price level. Both aggregate demand and aggregate supply shifts contribute to an overall increase in national output.
