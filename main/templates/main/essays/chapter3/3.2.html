Methods and Effects of Government Intervention in Markets

Unit 3.2 in Chapter 3: Government microeconomic intervention

ASSESS WHETHER BUS AND LOCAL RAIL SYSTEMS IN CITIES SHOULD RECEIVE SUBSTANTIAL SUBSIDIES FROM GOVERNMENTS.

SOURCE: CAIE, ECONOMICS, 9708, PAPER 2, VARIANT 3, OCTOBER / NOVEMBER, 2023, QUESTION 3B, 12 MARKS

VIDEO REFERENCE LINK

Model Answer written by Thura:

AO1 (4 MARKS)

A subsidy can be defined as support from the government either in financial or non-financial forms. Usually, a subsidy is given to increase the consumption or production of certain goods and services.

AO2 (4 MARKS)

A subsidy to bus and local rail systems in cities can be perceived as a merit good, as they help low-income families in terms of lower transportation costs, thereby allowing them to spend more on other necessities. Secondly, this can reduce road congestion and traffic, thereby improving labour mobility. Thirdly, subsidies to railway firms will allow citizens to switch from private cars, which are considered demerit goods exacerbating air pollution, to public transport, which is far more sustainable and does not contribute to air pollution. Therefore, governments should provide subsidies to bus and local rail systems.

However, there is an opportunity cost to providing subsidies. The provision of these subsidies means that governments have given up the opportunity to improve other important facilities such as education, healthcare, infrastructure, etc. Secondly, if the demand for bus and rail tickets are relatively price inelastic, then providing subsidies may not necessarily offer the aforementioned benefits. Likewise, during emergencies or if citizens prefer private cars to public transport for convenience/privacy reasons, people might not use public transport despite lower ticket prices due to subsidies.

AO3 (4 MARKS)

Therefore, government subsidies to bus and local rail systems can have both advantages and disadvantages. However, the benefits of providing subsidies to bus and local rail systems generally outweigh its downsides. And the degree of benefits may depend on the transportation preferences of the citizens of a particular city, and how much a government is willing to invest in public transport.
