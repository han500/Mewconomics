Addressing Income and Wealth Inequality

Unit 3.3 in Chapter 3: Government microeconomic intervention

A GOVERNMENT WISHES TO KEEP THE PRICE OF AN ESSENTIAL FOOD, SUCH AS RICE, AFFORDABLE TO HELP LOW-INCOME HOUSEHOLDS. ASSESS WHETHER A POLICY OF FIXING THE MAXIMUM PRICE FOR AN ESSENTIAL FOOD IS LIKELY TO BE MORE EFFECTIVE THAN A POLICY OF MAKING TRANSFER PAYMENTS TO LOW-INCOME HOUSEHOLDS.

SOURCE: CAIE, ECONOMICS, 9708, PAPER 2, VARIANT 2, MAY / JUNE, 2023, QUESTION 2B, 12 MARKS

Model Answer written by Thura:

AO1 (4 MARKS)
Definitions and examples

Effective maximum price is when the government sets a price below the market price to allow for more consumption. Transfer payments mean the direct payments by the government to those targeted income groups, usually low-income groups. Examples of transfer payments may include unemployment benefits, pensions, etc. The government may intervene in either way to help low-income households satisfy their basic necessities.

AO2 (4 MARKS)
Must be a two-sided response. Here, AO2 is structured by: the advantages and disadvantages of max price and the advantages and disadvantages of transfer payments

Effective maximum price, a price set below equilibrium price, will allow for the price of foods to be more affordable. Therefore, low-income earners, consuming them at a lower price, will have greater disposable income, thereby being able to buy other household necessities. Thus, their living standards will improve. Moreover, it incurs no direct costs to the government. Thus, the government can spend more of its budgets on improving education or supplying healthcare services, benefiting low-income groups. However, the maximum price on rice, for example, can cause a shortage in the market as more people can now afford to demand rice. Thus, it will lead to longer queues and allocative problems, thereby certain low-income earners might lose out. Furthermore, the maximum price policy may require additional government law enforcement to assure the policy is effectively benefiting people with low income and to prevent the formation of informal markets, thereby incurring extra costs to the government.

Thus, a government may choose to offer transfer payments instead. Transfer payments can be targeted at certain groups. Therefore, by providing unemployment benefits, for instance, the government can assist unemployed people in buying basic essentials such as rice, leading to a more equal society. Moreover, transfer payments provide flexibility in consumption, meaning low-income earners can use the government’s financial assistance for whatever essentials they need. Unlike the maximum price policy, which is effective only on certain goods, transfer payments can better help low-income earners by allowing them to allocate the funds based on their certain needs. However, transfer payments can be costly and have an opportunity cost. The government will have less budget to spend on education, infrastructure, or healthcare, which may benefit low-income groups more than transfer payments. Additionally, transfer payments may lower the incentives for low-income earners to work, thereby raising higher dependency on the government. Thus, the government might face higher risks and costs in the future from providing transfer payments.

AO3 (4 MARKS)
Evaluation

To conclude, if low-income earners are struggling to buy certain essential goods, then a maximum price policy might be applicable as it will directly influence the affordability of specific goods. However, if the government wants to help low-income earners in general or promote higher income equality, transfer payments might be more effective. Which method is more effective may depend on the specific needs of low-income groups in that particular economy, the financial capability of the government, and its objectives in the economy.
